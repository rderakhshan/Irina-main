How it works

From the roaster to your grinder in three short steps.

No warehouses, no year-old bags on a shelf, no guesswork. Here is exactly what happens after you order.

  1. 01

    Choose your coffee

    Pick a single origin from the shelf, or let a subscription match the roast schedule to your habits. Every lot lists its farm, altitude, process, and tasting notes, so the bag you open is never a mystery.

  2. 02

    We roast, rest, and record

    Your coffee is roasted to order in the Diedrich, rested for 48 to 72 hours, then cupped by the same roaster who dropped it. If a cup misses the notes, that batch is pulled and roasted again.

  3. 03

    It ships within 48 hours

    The sealed bag leaves our door within two days of roasting and usually lands on yours three to seven days off roast — the window when coffee is most aromatic, sweet, and alive.

Brew guide

Three ways to make the bag sing.

Great coffee forgives a lot, but the right recipe rewards you. Start with these numbers, then trust your palate more than the stopwatch.

V60 Pour-Over

A clean, bright cup that shows off floral and fruit notes. This is where our Ethiopian and Kenyan lots shine brightest.

Ratio
1:1622 g coffee : 352 g water
Grind
Medium-finelike table salt
Temperature
205°F / 96°Cjust off the boil
Time
3:00–3:30from first pour
  1. Rinse the paper filter with hot water, then discard the rinse water.
  2. Add your grounds, level the bed, and bloom with 50 g of water for 45 seconds.
  3. Pour in slow, even circles, keeping the water level steady until you reach 352 g.
  4. Give the dripper a gentle swirl, let it drain, and rest the cup for 30 seconds before sipping.

French Press

A forgiving, full-bodied brew that loves our chocolatey lots from Guatemala and Sumatra. Immersion extracts deep sweetness without fuss.

Ratio
1:1435 g coffee : 500 g water
Grind
Coarselike sea salt
Temperature
200°F / 93°Cabout 45 seconds off boil
Time
4:00plus a slow press
  1. Warm the press with hot water, then add the coarse grounds.
  2. Pour all 500 g of water in one steady pass and stir gently to wet every particle.
  3. Rest the lid on top, but do not plunge yet. Start a four-minute timer.
  4. Break the crust with a spoon, skim the foam, and press down slowly and evenly.

Espresso

Dialed shots reward you with syrupy, concentrated sweetness. Use a scale, distribute evenly, and taste every single pull.

Ratio
1:218 g in : 36 g out
Grind
Finelike caster sugar
Temperature
200°F / 93°Cmachine dependent
Time
26–30 secfrom first drop
  1. Grind directly into your portafilter, then distribute the bed with a few light taps.
  2. Tamp flat with firm, even pressure, and flush the group head before locking in.
  3. Start the shot and your timer together. Aim for 36 g of liquid in 26–30 seconds.
  4. If it runs fast, grind finer; if it stalls, grind coarser. Adjust one variable at a time.

The roastery

Small batches, obsessive records, one Diedrich IR-12.

Every lot starts as a sample on our cupping table. We buy green coffee directly from six partner farms, and before we commit to a single sack we measure moisture, screen the beans by size, and cup every arrival. A coffee has to score at least 84 points and, more importantly, make two of our three roasters reach for a second cup.

On roast days, our Diedrich IR-12 is charged to roughly 200°C before the green beans drop in. We watch the rate of rise minute by minute, stretch the Maillard phase to develop sweetness, and aim to finish each batch with a development-time ratio between 18% and 22%. A washed Ethiopian and a wet-hulled Sumatran never get the same curve — they get the curve their density and process are asking for.

After the drop, the beans hit the cooling tray within seconds, then rest for 48 to 72 hours so the gases settle and the cup stops tasting grassy. We brew a sample from every single batch with a refractometer beside the scale, and only then does the bag get stamped with its roast date and sealed with a one-way valve. If a batch tastes shy of our notes, it never ships.
